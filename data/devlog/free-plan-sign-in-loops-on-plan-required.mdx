Signing in to Cursor with a free (Hobby) account completes in the browser, and the client then reports it is not authenticated. Retry sign-in runs the whole browser flow again and lands in the same place. Swap to a paid account and the identical flow works first go. This is [pingdotgg/t3code#19766](https://github.com/pingdotgg/t3code/issues/19766).

## Why it happens

Cursor's local SDK requires a paid plan - [confirmed on Cursor's own forum](https://forum.cursor.com/t/agent-create-local-apikey-model-fails-for-free-users-because-local-model-validation-calls-cloud-v1-models/160839). A free-plan key is answered with `403 plan_required`, but the client's catalog check only treats `401` as an authentication failure. The plan rejection surfaces as a generic catalog error, the verification step discards the credential that was just created, and the account view shows a fixed "sign-in failed or expired" message that never mentions the plan. The user retries a flow that cannot succeed.

## What to do

Sign in with a paid account, or set `CURSOR_API_KEY` from one:

```bash
export CURSOR_API_KEY=<key-from-a-paid-account>
```

The wider lesson is worth keeping. When a sign-in reports failure immediately after a successful browser round trip, look at the status code before blaming the token. A `403` that is not `401` is usually the product saying no - a plan, a licence, an enterprise policy - and any client that folds every rejection into "your session expired" will send you chasing credentials that were never the problem. An enterprise account whose identity lookup returns `403` fails through the same path, so the same check applies there. The fix upstream is to surface the plan rejection instead of the generic message ([pingdotgg/t3code#15075](https://github.com/pingdotgg/t3code/issues/15075)).
