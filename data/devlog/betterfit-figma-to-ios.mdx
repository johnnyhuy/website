The BetterFit design work had moved on, but the iOS app was still carrying the
old ember-orange palette and earlier screen layouts. I used this pass to bring
the app back into agreement with the Figma direction.[^change]

_BetterFit's plan-design prototype, showing the exercise rows and yellow actions. This is a design preview rather than a capture of the shipped iOS app._

The identity is now electric yellow, black ink and neutral structure. Instead of
giving every status another colour, the yellow ladder uses brightness alongside
words. Glass is reserved for floating chrome, and yellow text on light surfaces
has its own darker token.

I also added the `BFLedger` primitives: section rules, readouts, rows, quick actions
and the other pieces needed to make the screens feel like one app. WorkoutHome
and ActiveSession moved onto those components, alongside the profile, recovery,
sign-in and settings surfaces.

## More than a palette swap

The workout plan now has inline weight and reps editing, superset pairing and
equipment swapping. The active session carries the same direction through set
logging, ordering and the rest bar.

This was the app-screens-to-code pass, not proof that every part of BetterFit was
finished. The PR also called out twelve pre-existing serious SwiftLint violations
in untouched files. Keeping that distinction visible was more useful than making
a design sync sound like a clean bill of health for the whole codebase.
