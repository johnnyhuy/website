I moved the S2000 dash preview towards something I could use as a bench tool,
rather than just a picture of the cluster.

This pass corrected the readout proportions and shared warning artwork, then
grouped the face, scenario and playback controls around the display. Telemetry is
readable without opening a JSON dump, and the raw JSON is still available when I
need it. Light, dark and system appearance settings persist between visits.[^change]

![Simulated AP1 cluster in the S2000 bench studio, with warning scenarios and playback controls](/images/devlog/s2000-bench.webp)

_The bench studio's AP1 preview with simulated telemetry and warning controls._

The separate display route hides the web controls and uses mock telemetry, so the
cluster can be previewed fullscreen on the Pi. It is a preview, not a claim that
the live car integration is finished.

## Looking at the physical setup

I also added a 3D workspace for inspecting and downloading the OLED/Pi enclosure
prototype. That puts the face design and its housing next to each other in the
workflow, instead of treating the enclosure as something to work out afterwards.

![Exploded 3D view of the front frame, display, enclosure and rear cover for the S2000 OLED and Raspberry Pi prototype](/images/devlog/s2000-enclosure.webp)

_Exploded CAD view of the OLED and Raspberry Pi enclosure prototype._

There is still an important physical unknown: the exact panel model, native
resolution and active area need recording. The target is a seven-inch OLED and
Raspberry Pi for the AP1, but the downloadable geometry is not evidence of a
measured fit in the car.

That boundary matters. The studio makes the design easier to inspect and iterate;
the hardware measurements still have to decide what gets built.
