I merged BetterFit's first Swift Package framework. It gave the strength-training
idea a concrete shape: exercises, workouts, templates and training plans, with
managers for plans, equipment swaps, recovery and social features.[^framework]

That structure mattered before the later screen work. A workout needed to remain
the same piece of data when it came from a template, had equipment swapped or
was passed to the tracking service.

## A starting point for the coach

The first tracking service accepted motion data and used magnitude thresholds
for repetitions and rest. The adaptation service applied rules based on workout
completion and progression, including volume changes and plateau detection.

Those were initial implementations to develop and test. The presence of an
`AIAdaptationService` class did not make it a trained model, and a threshold-based
rep detector was not proof of reliable watch tracking in a real workout.

The useful milestone was getting the models and service boundaries into one
framework. It made the next work more specific: improve the tracking, exercise
the adaptation rules and build an app experience around data that already had a
place to live.

[^framework]: [BetterFit's initial Swift framework](https://github.com/echohello-dev/betterfit/pull/1).
