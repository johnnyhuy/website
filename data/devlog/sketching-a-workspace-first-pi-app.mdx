I wrote out a desktop-app concept around Pi's small coding-agent runtime.[^pi]
The motivation was the friction around switching workspaces and getting back up
to speed, rather than adding another chat pane with a different skin.

The main screen I wanted was closer to a workbench: which repositories and
worktrees are active, what changed, what is stale or blocked, and which branch
owns the conversation.

## Recover the state of work

The initial bets were a workspace overview, meaningful diff previews, a context
recovery card and visible repository/task state. Provider configuration also
needed to be easier to inspect than a hidden collection of settings.

Performance was part of the question, not a result. I wanted to test whether long
conversations, mounted terminals, file trees and large diffs could stay responsive
without every background update disturbing the whole interface.

This was scope-setting research. The next useful step was a small prototype
around those workspace and recovery interactions, before expanding into broader
multi-runtime ambitions. Writing down the product boundary gave that prototype
something more specific to test than whether the app looked different.

Later work: [connecting Pidex to real Pi sessions](/devlog/pidex-real-session-resume) exercised session resume and streamed tool output in the desktop workbench.

[^pi]: [Pi's coding-agent runtime](https://pi.dev/).
