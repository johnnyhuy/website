# Bots Scale Down. Headcount Doesn't.

The capital barrier for building software is gone. The team barrier went with it.

You can run a neo enterprise on your own now. Free tiers, open source, AI tooling. What used to need a seed round and a co-founder needs attention and a long game.

Free isn't free though. You trade attention. Yours and everyone else's. The free stack carries the ops, the learning curve, the community load. AI tooling is what makes that trade survivable for a solo operator.

[Backstage](https://backstage.io) is the template. Spotify built it, [open sourced it](https://github.com/backstage/backstage), and then watched a whole ecosystem spin up around it. Enterprises adopted the free OSS, hit operational reality, and admitted they just wanted managed services. That's the leg in. If the product is genuinely good.

The next wave is software factories. Bots raise the PR, review it, approve and merge it. Not a demo any more.

[Lauren Tan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zc07HI9Ppxk) cleared the trust curve at Cursor and merged a thousand PRs in a month. [Amp](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uKO1ueC6eu0) ships fifty times a day and killed the pull request entirely.

The question I keep getting from engineers is deeper than quality: who's maintaining this stuff?

That question carries a bias toward engineering excellence. Stable systems. Code you can hand to the next person. Fair lens. It usually comes from people inside org structures built to reward that kind of knowledge work.

Here's the asymmetry. Large companies can't scale down manpower without layoffs. Bots scale down like cloud infrastructure. Turn the dial and the spend goes.

A small company incubating and shipping feature work without much toil never needed that payroll to begin with.

Enterprises hate seeing people idle. So busy work becomes the default. Normalised for decades. When the work is orchestrating and sharing context to a squad of bots, there's just less work. You can ramp bots down. Ramp people down and it's called a restructure.

I've watched engineers go through the stages of grief on this. Some can't get past code completion. "I just want it to generate unit tests. I don't trust it. I don't want my day job to be prompting." That's denial. Monolithic PRs were never a good idea. Using them for feature work is not it.

[The Phoenix Project](https://www.amazon.com/Phoenix-Project-DevOps-Helping-Business/dp/1942788290) and [The Goal](https://www.amazon.com/Goal-Process-Ongoing-Improvement/dp/0884271951) nailed this decades ago. Agile and lean were always about flow, not headcount.

Parts of my SWE profession look like a hobby now. Mine started as one. Building websites for gaming communities. Tinkering. Generalist is great for expanding knowledge but you miss the parts that used to be specialties. Cloud. Backend. The deep stuff.
